The Code Less Taken is an independent technical publication about uncommon languages, forgotten systems, underused techniques, and alternative computing. It asks what these approaches made possible, why they stayed outside the mainstream, and what present-day engineers can learn from them.

Longer investigations combine primary sources, technical reconstruction, modern comparison, and an account of the forces that shaped adoption. Shorter field notes examine a single tool, paper, protocol, build, or mechanism.

## Who it is for

We write for engineers, researchers, technical leaders, specialist practitioners, advanced learners, and anyone curious about how computing might have developed differently. You do not need prior knowledge of the subject; unfamiliar terms are explained without flattening the technical detail.

## Editorial position

Low adoption does not prove that an idea was technically inferior. Obscurity does not prove that it was secretly better, either. We examine technical limits alongside economics, standards, timing, tooling, and institutional support.

The publication does not accept editorial control from sponsors, members, vendors, or partners. Material relationships will be disclosed with the work they support.
