Software history is not a clean sequence of better ideas replacing worse ones. Tooling, standards, hiring, timing, and institutional support help determine which systems become defaults.

Some alternatives failed for technical reasons. Others arrived before the hardware or institutions needed to sustain them. A few survive in specialist domains because their guarantees remain difficult to reproduce.

The useful questions are concrete: What did a system make possible? What did it cost? Why did adoption stall? Does its central mechanism transfer to present conditions?

## How we investigate

Each investigation begins with a bounded question and primary material: manuals, papers, source code, recorded talks, interviews, standards, and contemporary accounts. Secondary sources help locate the record but do not replace it.

When a claim can be tested, we reconstruct enough of the system to expose its mechanism. That may require repairing a build, reproducing an environment, writing a clean-room implementation, or documenting why reproduction is no longer possible.

Comparison criteria are set before conclusions. A modern baseline should test the claimed advantage without hiding differences in hardware, workload, correctness, or operating context.

Published work separates verified fact, interpretation, and speculation. Versions, deviations, licenses, limits, review status, and corrections remain visible.

## What we cover

The Code Less Taken covers uncommon languages, divergent programming models, forgotten systems and protocols, durable legacy platforms, underused techniques, failed technologies, recovered environments, and older ideas returning under new names.

Obscurity alone is not a reason to care. Coverage centers on subjects that offer a transferable design insight, an inspectable artifact, an unresolved technical question, or a credible modern application. This is not a nostalgia publication.

## What you can expect

A substantial investigation explains the mechanism, shows the evidence, and states which trade-offs transfer to current work. Its conclusion may support the mainstream choice, identify a narrow use for the alternative, or find that the idea no longer holds up.

The archive will grow slowly. Depth, reproducibility, and maintenance matter more than publishing volume.
