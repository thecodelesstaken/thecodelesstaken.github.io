This page explains how The Code Less Taken supports technical and historical claims. Each substantial investigation begins with a research question and evaluation criteria.

## Topic choice

A topic is worth covering when an uncommon, abandoned, specialized, or historically divergent approach can reveal a transferable insight, an inspectable artifact, an unresolved question, or a credible present-day application.

## Sources

Claims draw first from primary documentation, source code, papers, manuals, standards, recorded talks, and interviews. Contemporary evidence is distinguished from retrospective accounts. Sources are linked where access and rights permit.

## Reconstruction

When a claim depends on implementation, the relevant code, environment, dataset, versions, instructions, licenses, and deviations are published whenever practical. Failed reconstructions are documented rather than omitted.

## Comparison

Evaluation criteria and the modern baseline are chosen before conclusions. Comparisons account for correctness, usability, performance, economics, maintainability, and institutional fit where relevant. Benchmark limits are part of the result.

## Review and uncertainty

Major technical and historical claims receive specialist review when qualified reviewers are available. Published work distinguishes verified fact, interpretation, and speculation. Missing evidence remains an open question rather than being forced into a tidy narrative.

## After publication

Living guides and executable artifacts carry version and maintenance information. Material corrections remain visible. A publication date marks the release of an investigation, not the end of its record.
